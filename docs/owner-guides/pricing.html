Pricing guide

How you set your prices in the back office — and exactly where each one shows up for the customer.

This is an onboarding walkthrough. Do the steps in order; each one shows what the customer sees on the website as a result. You never edit code or prices in two places — everything here is set from Dashboard and takes effect on the storefront immediately.

1 · How pricing works

You sell a ride in one of three ways. Every fare is built in this order: base fare → time surcharge → event-date uplift → meet & greet → minimum fare → tax. (The exception is a city-to-city fixed route, which is an all-in flat price and skips the rest.)

Way to priceWhat it isWhere you set it
Point-to-point (per-mile)Price grows with distance, using each vehicle's rate card.Vehicles & rates → the vehicle's distance bands
Hourly hirePrice = hourly rate × hours booked.Vehicles & rates → the vehicle's hourly rate
City-to-city (fixed)A flat, all-in price per vehicle for a named route (e.g. Austin ↔ San Antonio).City routes

2 · First-time setup, step by step

Each step: what you do in the dashboard → where it appears for the customer.

  1. Add your vehicles

    Dashboard
    Vehicles & rates → Add vehicle: name, description, passenger & luggage capacity, display order, and upload a photo.
    Customer sees
    The vehicle appears on the Fleet page and as a choice at checkout — with the photo you uploaded.

  2. Set the per-mile rate card (distance bands)

    Dashboard
    On the vehicle, add distance bands. They must start at 0 miles, be continuous (no gaps), and the last band is left open-ended. Example: 0–6 mi @ $15.00/mi, then 6+ mi @ $2.90/mi.
    Customer sees
    When they enter a pickup & drop-off, the quote for that vehicle is calculated from these bands.

  3. Set the hourly rate

    Dashboard
    On the vehicle, set Hourly rate (e.g. $85.00/hour).
    Customer sees
    When they pick By the hour and a number of hours, the price is rate × hours.

  4. Meet & greet fee

    Dashboard
    On the vehicle, set Meet & greet fee (e.g. $25.00).
    Customer sees
    If they tick Meet & Greet, this is added as its own line (except on city-to-city, which is all-in).

  5. Minimum fare

    Dashboard
    On the vehicle, set Minimum fare (e.g. $75.00).
    Customer sees
    Very short trips never quote below this floor.

  6. Time surcharges

    Dashboard
    Time surcharges → add a window, e.g. Late night 21:00–06:00, +20%.
    Customer sees
    A late-night pickup quote goes up, shown as its own "+20%" line.

  7. Event dates

    Dashboard
    Event dates → add a date with an uplift, e.g. SXSW +40%.
    Customer sees
    Pickups on that date are priced higher.

  8. Tax

    Dashboard
    Tax & rules → set the tax rate (0 turns tax off).
    Customer sees
    A tax line on the quote and the receipt.

  9. City routes (fixed intercity prices)

    Dashboard
    City routes → add a route (e.g. Austin ↔ San Antonio, both ways) and a flat price per vehicle.
    Customer sees
    When their pickup & drop-off cities match the route, the flat price replaces the per-mile fare — automatically, nothing extra to pick.

3 · The three offerings, with worked examples

Point-to-point transfer (per-mile)

Used whenever the customer enters a pickup and a drop-off. The distance is measured by Google; the fare walks the rate card band by band.

Example — Business Class, AUS Airport → The Domain (~18 mi), daytime
First 6 mi × $15.00$90.00
Next 12 mi × $2.90$34.79
Total$124.79
A 23:00 pickup would add a late-night +20% line; a meet & greet would add its fee; tax (if set) applies last.

Hourly hire

Used when the customer picks By the hour. Price is simply the hourly rate × hours, then the same surcharge / meet-&-greet / minimum / tax rules on top.

Example — Business Class at $85.00/hour, 4 hours
4 h × $85.00$340.00
Total$340.00

Watch: a customer books by the hour

City-to-city (fixed route)

An all-in flat price per vehicle for a named route. It replaces the per-mile fare and does not add surcharges or tax. Set it once in City routes; the website applies it automatically when the pickup & drop-off cities match.

Example — Austin ↔ San Antonio (both directions)
VehicleFlat price (all-in)
Business Class$200.00
Business SUV$260.00
Chrysler 300 Limousine$400.00
A vehicle you don't price on the route simply falls back to its normal per-mile fare for that trip.

4 · Which price wins

  • City-to-city beats per-mile when a trip matches a route and that vehicle has a route price. Otherwise the vehicle is priced per-mile as usual.
  • Transfer vs hourly is chosen by the customer (drop-off address = transfer; By the hour = hourly).
  • Surcharges and tax apply to transfer & hourly, never to city-to-city (that price is all-in).
City matching note. City-to-city matches on the city (e.g. Austin ↔ San Antonio). A trip starting in a suburb (say, Round Rock) won't match the Austin route unless you add it as its own route. For specific neighbourhoods, add more routes.

5 · Check your prices before you rely on them

  1. Open the website, enter a pickup & drop-off (or By the hour), and run a quote — you see exactly what a customer sees.
  2. Change a rate card or a city-route price in the dashboard, re-run the quote, and confirm the number moved.
  3. Common gotchas: a gap in the distance bands (those miles price at $0), a vehicle with no bands and no hourly rate (it won't quote online), or a city-route price missing for a vehicle (it falls back to per-mile).
